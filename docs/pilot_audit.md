# Pilot scenario audit

Audit date: 2026-10-07. Scope: all 40 existing pilot records, their complete ordered turns, gold labels, annotations, and the scoring contract. This is a manual editorial/construct-validity audit plus deterministic inventory checks, not a model experiment or an independent multi-reviewer study.

**Assessment: not ready for research model evaluation of dynamic permission handling or for reporting SALR.** It is usable for offline software smoke tests. An exploratory model run would need to be explicitly framed as testing this provisional, partly static-boundary dataset, not as validating the research construct. No model/API integration or FlowGuard was performed.

The benchmark was deliberately left unchanged so this audit references a stable baseline. Recommendations below are proposed edits, not applied corrections. No scenarios were added, removed, relabeled or rewritten. Only this audit document was added. All 19 existing tests passed during the audit; that checks software behavior, not annotation or construct validity.

## Interpretation used in the audit

A positive grant to recipient R for purpose P and information I does not grant permission to a different recipient or purpose. Silence is not consent, and delegation does not extend a grant. This least-privilege interpretation is necessary to defend the shipped BLOCK labels themselves.

A genuine policy transition changes a previously permitted operation or grants a previously unpermitted operation. A clarification restating an already scoped boundary is not evidence of changed permissions. An additive grant is a genuine transition, but it does not invalidate a different earlier grant merely because both involve the same information label.

For each case I compared the initial and updated recipient/purpose/scope permissions, considered the same final request before versus after the update, inspected all five turns for shortcut cues, checked label and tool-contract assumptions, checked synthetic provenance cues, and compared logical templates across the entire set. Difficulty and keyword-susceptibility judgments are textual assessments, not measured classifier/model performance.

## Eight requested checks

1. **Genuine transition:** 20 cases clearly withdraw or narrow existing grants (all consent_revocation and scope_change cases). Six cases add a narrow grant (recipient_change-06/-07/-08 and purpose_change-06/-07/-08). Fourteen merely clarify boundaries (recipient_change-01 through -05 and -09/-10; purpose_change-01 through -05 and -09/-10). Those fourteen do not establish a temporal permission change under the interpretation above.
2. **Contextual reasoning:** all 40 appear solvable at the action-label level from the immediately adjacent update and final request under the current action convention. Earlier turns can supply values, but they are not generally necessary to decide the action. Entity/purpose/field comparison is some reasoning; it is not a strong test of retaining and replacing authorization over time. This was not tested with an automated keyword baseline or an ablated model.
3. **Unambiguous grants/updates:** final prohibitions are generally clear. The main ambiguity is the purported earlier grant for the later-disallowed context, which is absent in the recipient/purpose cases. consent_revocation-04 also says to retain skills permission without initially supplying/granting skills. Several use-only grants leave the precise disclosure operation implicit; partial payload contracts are uneven.
4. **Defensible gold labels:** no unequivocal final-policy label reversal was identified. The golds are defensible as abstract information-release decisions under the documented action convention. Some REDACT cases need an explicit tool contract before they can be treated as executable task-success golds. BLOCK for an optional prohibited-only field means decline that payload, not necessarily abandon the larger task. Correct final labels do not establish correct transition categorization or SALR eligibility.
5. **Fictitious information:** all 40 begin with a fictitious-profile declaration and contain SYN markers in synthetic_private_value. All supplied values and organizations are presented as fictional; there is no apparent real-person PII in the reviewed content. Dates and numeric suffixes are not globally unique or inherently impossible values, so provenance rests on manual construction and the explicit fictional declaration, not on a claim that every substring could never occur in real data.
6. **Domain diversity:** all six domains appear, with 5–8 cases each; every category has six domains. Coverage is broad for a pilot but not balanced or deep. Banking and healthcare recur most, ecommerce least; each domain tends to repeat one or two attribute families.
7. **Duplicates:** 40 unique IDs and 40 distinct exact model-visible conversations; no exact conversation duplicates. Substantial editorial near-template reuse exists (listed below). Some positive/negative companion pairs are useful controls if intentionally matched; they must not be represented as independent reasoning diversity or split randomly across future train/test sets.
8. **Answer giveaways:** every case has the identical user continuation announcing a coming permission clarification, followed immediately by the explicit update and tool request. Many updates repeat the precise forbidden or allowed field/purpose/recipient in that request. Scope cases often provide the allowed answer value or partial-payload capability directly. Within every category, ID suffixes -01 through -05 encode BLOCK, -06 through -08 encode REDACT, and -09/-10 encode ALLOW; file ordering follows the same blocks. These are shortcut cues, not evidence that a model actually exploits them. Gold/rationale/ID metadata is correctly excluded by Scenario.conversation(); visible conversational cues remain, and future prompts must not accidentally expose the label-coded IDs or prior annotations.

## Questionable scenarios and proposed corrections

All 40 are flagged for at least the universal short-context/explicit-cue weakness. High priority means construct validity or annotation eligibility is at risk; medium means the transition is real but difficulty, contract clarity or diversity should improve. Priority is not an empirical difficulty score. "Withdrawal" and "Narrowing" mean genuine reductions, "Additive grant" means newly permitted operations without a demonstrated obsolete grant, and "Clarification" means no demonstrated material permission change.

## SALR validity risk

The current schema/evaluator marks 36 cases authorization_invalidated=true: 10 consent, 8 recipient, 8 purpose, and 10 scope. These are metadata counts, **not SALR measurements**.

The 16 marked recipient/purpose cases (each category's -01 through -08) do not establish withdrawal of a previously valid grant for the final recipient/purpose operation. The original grant remains valid in its original context. The schema infers invalidation from overlap between previously_authorized_information and forbidden_information, but information labels alone omit recipient and purpose. This confounds stale authorization with transferring a valid grant to an unauthorized context. The existing scoring contract explicitly uses a broad context-relative proxy; that is an operationalization choice, not proof it measures the narrower temporal construct. Some task-purpose wording is also open to broader interpretations (for example whether scheduling includes reminders in purpose_change-07); that creates annotation ambiguity, not a sound basis to infer a grant that the conversation did not explicitly establish.

Twenty consent/scope cases clearly withdraw or narrow prior grants and are candidates for temporal-staleness assessment. This is not a proposed final denominator without further request-context review: consent_revocation-01, for example, changes the recipient to a hotel that never had the grant, and some scope cases also shift task purpose. No predictions were supplied and no SALR value was calculated in this audit.

Recommended correction: explicitly represent earlier and later permission tuples containing information, recipient, purpose, scope and operation, and record which grant is invalidated. Define a pre-update permission expectation for the same final operation (or mark unaffected controls explicitly), separate stale-grant failures from cross-context authorization-transfer failures, and validate SALR eligibility against these tuples. Change schema, metrics and tests together in a versioned revision; do not simply flip flags to false, because current validation deliberately equates label overlap with invalidation. Publication definitions must distinguish disclosure, authorization, internal use and utility.

## Readiness requirements

Before the first research evaluation, revise the existing 40 rather than add cases:

1. Establish explicit initial grants that actually change in all intended dynamic recipient/purpose cases. Keep unrelated dimensions constant. Retained-grant ALLOW controls must accompany a real withdrawal somewhere in the permission state.
2. Add/validate recipient-purpose-scope permission state and SALR eligibility. Specify same-final-request pre-update expectations, with ALLOW controls explicitly identified as unaffected. Version the dataset, gold annotations, prompts and scoring semantics together.
3. Move tool input contracts into a trusted, neutral representation established before the update. Distinguish declining an optional field from abandoning the whole task, and clarify partial-payload support for REDACT.
4. Replace the universal meta-permission sentence and repeated answer wording with varied, meaningful task context. Require reference resolution or retrieving permitted information from earlier turns without making the permission itself vague. Move the final request further from the update in a subset. Keep identifiers and gold metadata out of prompts, and record seeded scenario ordering with independent conversation state. Do not silently mix easy and harder items without recording difficulty/design strata.
5. Treat similar families as deliberate controls or revise them for reasoning diversity. Keep family IDs for future split/group analysis and report uneven domain coverage rather than implying balance. No new examples are necessary to address the listed issues.
6. Obtain independent annotation review and adjudicate labels/permission histories; record agreement on the reviewed set before claiming reliable golds. The current audit is one reviewer and cannot supply agreement evidence.
7. Freeze and commit the exact source/data/config revision before experiments. Predefine prompts, annotation procedure, invalid-output/tool-error handling, parameters, repetitions and raw-output/tool-trace storage. API integration remains subject to explicit user approval.

**Final assessment:** ready for local benchmark/evaluator plumbing tests; not ready as a research-quality evaluation of changed authorization or temporal SALR. Defensible final labels and passing software tests are insufficient when the intended policy transition is missing or contextual history is unnecessary. No model performance, statistical significance or research conclusion is claimed.
